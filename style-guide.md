# Mark G. English — Version B style guide

## Direction
An approachable language-school poster: strong typography, a navy foundation and vivid yellow conversation shapes. The visitor's need leads; Mark's Cambridge qualification supports it.

## Colour
- Navy #11233B: headings, body text, dark sections and QR modules.
- Yellow #FFDC36: primary calls to action, emphasis and sticker background.
- White #FFFFFF: reading surfaces and the entire QR quiet zone.
- Muted text #516174: secondary copy on white.
- Divider #DCE2E9: subtle section separation.

## Typography and layout
Use Arial or Helvetica, with system fallbacks and no external font dependency. Bold, closely spaced headings; normal body text at 16–18 pixels. Maximum content width 1120 pixels. Mobile layouts stack naturally. Buttons are at least 50 pixels tall and have visible keyboard focus.

## Voice
Personal, direct and specific. Lead with “Need help with English?” and the four services: reading, writing, pronunciation and translation. State “Cambridge graduate English tutor” as a personal qualification. Do not imply university affiliation or accreditation. Do not invent reviews, prices, availability, trial offers or promised outcomes.

## Imagery
Speech bubbles are decorative graphics. The supplied Monica and Dirk image remains a secondary image below the text, not a portrait of Mark.

## Contact and measurement
Email links prefill “English enquiry - Version B”. SMS links identify page B in the editable draft. No message is sent automatically. Count enquiries mentioning B manually; clients may remove the marker, and social messages are not attributed. No analytics or cookies are added. Distribute comparable quantities of A/B stickers under similar conditions. This compares the sticker-and-page packages, not individual elements.

## QR artwork
Version B must encode its own live URL. Use a dark-on-white QR with at least four modules of quiet zone and Q-level error correction. Keep artistic graphics outside the QR. Deliver vector PDF/SVG and high-resolution PNG. Verify decoding from the final layout and test a physical sample before mass printing.
